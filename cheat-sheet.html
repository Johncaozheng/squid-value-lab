Squid preflop cheat sheet v2 (round 3: head fish, single straddle)

25/25 + a 50 straddle (STR) every hand, 30,000 stacks, rake on. Opens are 600 or 900. (n) is your squid bounty in chips: what winning this pot's squid is worth to you, against an average other player winning it. First-in % assume it's folded to you; "play" means limp or open. Each situation is weighted by how often it comes up (its share of all hands is in the heading). [R] robust: the advice still holds if every EV in the spot moves by 1 BB (25 chips). [M] model-dependent: a near tie, a rare spot, or a defence number. Source: research/round3/cheat_sheet_v2_output.txt (2,264 solves).

Fresh round (1,043): 11.5% of hands

  • The head fish makes the first pot worth 1,043: its winner gains 913 for the round, and every other seat loses 130. [R]
  • UTG+1 to CO: limp, don't open. Opening costs 108–146 a hand on average against limping. [R]
  • Play 38% UTG+1, 43% LJ, 51% HJ, 62% CO, all limps. The cut-off is soft: at UTG+1, a 25-chip error moves it anywhere from 22% to 64%. [M]
  • UTG+1 limps 22+, A2s+, K2s+, Q6s+, J7s+, T7s+, 97s+, 87s, 76s, A2o+, K8o+, Q9o+, J9o+. [M]
  • BTN: play every hand [R]; open 93%, at 900 with 69% of hands [M].
  • Facing a BTN 600 open, the STR never folds (3-bets 9%) and the BB folds 34%. [M] rare (0.3% / 0.8% of hands)

After the head fish, one player on 2 (zeros 674, the player on 2 880): 11.5%

  • Zeros tighten a little: UTG+1 plays 28% (38% fresh), dropping A2o–A6o, K2s, K8o, J7s, 97s, 87s and 76s. [M]
  • Still limp, don't open from UTG+1 to CO. [R] The BTN opens 92%, now mostly 600 (900 with only 38%). [M]
  • The player on 2 plays as in a fresh round: 39% UTG+1, 61% CO, and opens 99% on the BTN. [M]

Mid-round, holders vs zeros: 42% of hands, the most common spot

  • On 0 (786): play 38% UTG+1, 43% LJ, 51% HJ, 63% CO, mostly limps; the 4–12% who open always use 900. [M]
  • On 0, BTN: play every hand [R]; open 98%, at 900 with 65% [M]. In the straddle, fold only 8–10% to a BTN 600 open. [M]
  • Holding 1–2 (397): play about half as much: 19% UTG+1 (55+, A7s+, A5s, K9s+, Q9s+, JTs, ATo+, KTo+, QTo+), 37% CO. [M]
  • Holding, BTN: fold the worst 16% and open 64%, mostly 600. [M]
  • Holding, in the straddle: fold 58% to a holder's BTN open but only 28% to a zero's. In the BB: 75% and 57%. [M]

Someone on 3: 20.5% of hands

  • On 3 yourself (968, from 429 to 1,616): play like a zero, a bit looser: 46% UTG+1, adding Q2s–Q4s, J4s–J6s, Q8o and K7o. [M]
  • Zeros (943) play as mid-round zeros: 40% UTG+1, 67% CO, and the BTN opens 96%. Holders (363) tighten to 16% at UTG+1. [M]

Someone on 4: 5.5% of hands

  • On 4 (2,630, from 1,907 to 3,594): never fold first in from UTG+1 to the SB (2% or less). [R] Early, mostly limp: UTG+1 limps 75% and opens 22% (almost always 900); the CO opens 37%. [M]
  • On 4, BTN: open every hand to 900. [R] In the STR or BB, never fold to a BTN 600 open. [M]
  • On 0 (1,067): play as fresh: 38% UTG+1, 63% CO, and the BTN opens 84%. [M] Against the hunter's BTN 600 open, the STR folds 7% and 3-bets 17%. [M] very rare
  • Holding (390): you're a bystander, so tighten to 27% at UTG+1. [M]

Last two zeros (Z = 2): 9.2% of hands

  • You're one of them (933 with 7–8 squids out, 1,100 on the last squid): play 68–69% UTG+1 (opening 18–19%, always 900), 79–82% HJ, 86% CO. [M]
  • The BTN opens 98–100%, almost all to 900. [R] The SB opens 16–17%. [M]
  • Facing the other zero's BTN 600 open, the STR folds 0–2% and 3-bets 19%. Against a holder's open, the STR folds 4% and the BB 34–36%. [M]
  • 7–8 out and the last squid play the same, although the bounty differs (933 vs 1,100). [M]
  • You hold (203–240): fold most hands: play 15–18% at UTG+1 (66+, A8s+, K9s+, QTs+, JTs, ATo+, KJo+, QJo), and fold 18–21% on the BTN. [M]
  • Holding, facing a holder's BTN 600 open: the STR folds 66–71% and the BB 81–84%. [M]
  • One zero left never plays a hand: the round settles at once. [R]

Honest limits

  • Postflop is not solved. Every EV leans on our equity-realisation model.
  • We assume a single straddle every hand. The 100 double straddle comes in about 40–50% of real hands, and we have not solved it under the head fish.
  • Early-seat limp vs open is a near tie for premiums. At UTG+1, AA is worth 2,261 limped against 2,270 opened, so opening AA–QQ or AK costs under 30 chips. A cold-started solve of a nearby state did open some premiums early.
  • SB/BB first-in spots are rare and less certain: in a fresh round it is almost never folded to the SB (0.00% of hands). Every defence number sits at a spot reached in under 7% of hands.
  • 14% of hands have no close solve (the page shows them as "rough"): about 7% in the on-3 states and 4% with someone on 4, where only a solve one squid off may stand in. Solve accuracy: median 4.8 chips a hand, worst 10.0.
